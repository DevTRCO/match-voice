# Evaluation

Generated by `python -m matchvoice.evaluation`. Do not edit by hand; the machine-readable numbers are in `data/eval/results.json`. No model call and no key is used: the pipeline runs in replay mode, unpaced, on the recorded analyst lines.

## What is measured

- Setup: 3 synthetic matches (comeback-7, red_card-7, unlucky-7), 6 channels (en, de, fr by beginner, expert), 818 spoken lines at x30. A second run in real time (x1) shows silence and latency as a viewer lives them.

- **Grounding rate**: share of spoken lines that the deterministic verifier accepts when it checks the final text again with the facts of its moment. Reported with the share of lines written by the analyst, as opposed to a template (including a shorter template wording the director chose to fit the time).

- **Injected errors**: each spoken line is also corrupted (scoreline swapped, a number changed, a player named who is not part of the moment) and the verifier is asked again. This tests the verifier, not the commentary.

- **Key-moment coverage**: the importance-weighted share of `truth.json` moments the pipeline found (a moment of the matching kind and side within 30 s for goals and red cards; for a momentum swing, anywhere in its stretch from the start to the end the simulator wrote, with 60 s of lead), and the share a channel actually voiced.

- **Latency**: the analyst's ready delay, from the detector queueing a moment to the publisher releasing its lines, in viewer seconds (match seconds divided by speed), and the time from the event to the start of the line.

- **Speech time and silence**: share of match time a channel is speaking, and the gaps between lines.

## Weaknesses

1. Most spoken lines are templates. The analyst wrote 9.9% of all spoken lines at x30 and 3.4% in real time. Even among analyst-voice lines, only 40% (x30) and 45% (real time) carry the model's text, because the director often picks a shorter template wording to fit the time left. The newsroom published 100% of the lines it was asked for as analyst-written; the loss happens at scheduling, not in the newsroom.
2. Some truth moments are not found. 1 of 12 (6.2% importance-weighted) have no same-side moment of the matching kind in their window: momentum swing H in comeback-7 (3240 to 5580 s). The nearest one is 435 s outside it (a swing counts inside its stretch, plus a lead of 60 s). Of 27 goal, red card and momentum moments the pipeline produced, 19 match a truth moment; the rest are momentum phases the simulator did not label, which are not necessarily wrong.
3. Some detected moments get no analysis: red card in red_card-7. On every channel the analyst voice never spoke for these kinds (red card).
4. Analysis arrives late. The analyst's lines are ready a median 6.2 s (p95 7.9 s) after the moment. Lines the analyst wrote start a median 8.8 s (p95 19.3 s) of viewer time after the event at x30, template lines in the analyst voice a median 10.3 s, and play-by-play 2.3 s. These are the model latencies recorded when the replays were made, not measured in this run.
5. Silence only exists in real time. At x30 a line's speaking time is multiplied by the speed, so every channel speaks 100% of the match. In real time the channels speak 16.6% of the match, with a longest silence of 174.0 s and about 91 gaps over 20 s per channel and match. Speaking times are estimates from text length, not measured audio.
6. Grounding is a narrow check. The verifier tests numbers, scorelines, player names, language and length. It cannot tell whether a claim such as 'dominating' or 'against the run of play' is fair. The 100.0% grounding rate is an independent check on template lines (100.0% of 737), which the pipeline never verifies, and a self-check on analyst lines (100.0% of 81), which the newsroom already filtered with the same verifier. The injected-error test above is the better signal: score swapped 100.0% of 53, number changed 100.0% of 270, player swapped 99.8% of 589 caught. Missed: player swapped 'Abaras Tor in der 14. Minute (0-1) bei 0,25 xG erhöht die Siegwahrscheinlichkeit von Marlow Bay von 61 auf 84 Prozent.' (de/expert, comeback-7). The verifier does not see a surname with a suffix attached, such as a German genitive; the fix belongs in the verifier, not in this harness.
7. Small sample. Three synthetic matches, one seed each, and the truth file comes from the same simulator that produced the events, so coverage measures agreement with a simulator label, not with a human editor. German and French wording has not been read by native speakers.
